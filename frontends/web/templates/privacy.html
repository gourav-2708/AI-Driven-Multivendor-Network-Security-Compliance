net-audit checks the configuration files of network devices (routers, switches and firewalls) against security rules and compliance frameworks. This page explains what personal and customer information this service keeps, why, who can see it, how long it is kept, and what you can ask for. It describes how the software on this server actually works.

1. Who is responsible

This service is run by {{ policy.operator or 'the operator of this server' }}, which decides how the information described here is used. {% if policy.contact %}Privacy questions and requests: {{ policy.contact }}.{% endif %}

Each organization that uses the service decides what it uploads and who in it gets an account. Its administrators manage its users, data and integrations.

3. What we never keep

  • Configuration files. Files you upload, or that are fetched from a source, are written to a temporary folder, audited, and deleted as soon as the audit finishes, whether it worked or not. Only the report is kept.
  • Readable passwords. Your password is stored only as a salted hash. API tokens, password-reset links and invitation links are stored only as hashes.
  • Secrets from your configurations. Reports do not contain passwords, keys or whole configuration lines. An SNMP community string that is not a well-known default is cut to its first two characters.
  • Tracking or analytics. The service sends no usage data or telemetry anywhere, shows no advertising, and does not sell or rent information to anyone.

4. Who can see it

  • Your organization. Signed-in users and API tokens can see only their own organization's information. Viewers can read; auditors can also run audits and work on findings; administrators can also manage users, integrations and the audit log, and delete data.
  • The operator. Whoever runs this server and its database can technically read everything stored in it, and does so only to run, secure and support the service.
  • Service providers. {{ policy.hosting }}
  • Services your administrators connect.   • When the law requires it. The operator may disclose information if a valid legal order requires it.

5. How it is protected

  • Passwords, tokens and one-time links are stored only as hashes.
  • The session cookie is HTTP-only{% if policy.secure_cookie %}, sent only over HTTPS{% endif %}, and every form is protected against cross-site request forgery.
  • An account is locked for {{ policy.lock_minutes }} minutes after {{ policy.max_failures }} failed sign-ins.
  • Every request is checked against the user's organization and role. Changing the control register needs an administrator to re-enter their password.
  • Credentials entered for integrations are stored as entered, because they must be sent to the other system; they are never shown again or returned by the API. Administrators should give integrations accounts with the least access they need.

6. How long it is kept

  • Configuration files: only for the length of the audit.
  • Audit reports: until an administrator deletes them.
  • Devices, their score history, findings and alerts: until an administrator removes the device.
  • Integration settings and API tokens: until an administrator removes or revokes them.
  • Accounts, organizations and the audit log: kept while the service is used. There is no self-service deletion of these yet and no automatic expiry; ask for deletion as described below.

7. Your choices and rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to see, correct, export or delete your personal information, or object to how it is used.

  • See and export: reports can be downloaded as JSON, CSV, text and PDF, and administrators can download the evidence pack and the audit log.
  • Correct: change your password in Settings; ask an administrator to correct other details.
  • Delete: administrators can delete audits and devices themselves. To delete an account, an organization or audit-log entries, contact {{ policy.contact or 'the operator through your organization’s administrator' }}.

8. Other things to know

  • The service is meant for organizations and their staff, not for children.
  • Self-hosted copies of net-audit are run by the organization that installed them; this policy describes how the software behaves, and that organization is responsible for its own copy.
  • If this policy changes, the date at the top changes too.
